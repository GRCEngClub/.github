# AJ's LinkedIn voice

AJ's posts are short, fast, and unbothered. They land because of what he leaves
out: no defense, no lesson, no call to action. The single most common way to get
this wrong is to write a competent LinkedIn post — setup, insight, takeaway,
question for the audience. That is the opposite of the voice.

Read `references/voice.md` before drafting and `references/examples.md` for the
annotated real posts. Both are short.

## Workflow

### 1. Get the actual thing that happened
The post is always anchored to one concrete event: a Reddit thread, a launch, a
number, a message someone sent him. Ask for it if it isn't obvious. Do not
invent the event, the reaction, or who said what.

Ask for the screenshot too, if there is one. AJ's posts often carry the evidence
as an image and never re-explain it in the copy.

### 2. Find the reference
Most posts turn on one outside reference — a song lyric, a sports take, a Bible
beat — that carries the argument so he doesn't have to make it himself. If the
user gave you one (a lyrics screenshot, a song title), use it and quote it
exactly as the source spells it. If they didn't, either pull from what they've
told you or leave the post at three lines. A forced reference is worse than none.

### 3. Draft
Four short paragraphs is the standard shape. One breath each, blank line between
every one. Cold open, plain middle, hard close. Details in `references/voice.md`.

### 4. Run the cut list
Go through the "Cut these" section of `references/voice.md` line by line against
your draft. Every item on it is something a language model adds by reflex and AJ
never types. This pass usually removes 30–40% of a first draft, and that is the
draft getting better.

### 5. Deliver
Hand back the post as plain text, ready to paste — not in a code block, not with
commentary interleaved. If you had to guess at a fact, flag it in one line
underneath. Offer a second angle if you have a genuinely different one.

## Guardrails

- **Draft only.** Never post, schedule, or publish anything to LinkedIn.
- **Don't invent.** No made-up numbers, quotes, DMs, or reactions.
- **Never name the critic.** The posture depends on him not punching down. Don't
  name the person, link the thread, screenshot the commenter, or quote what they
  said. Report that it happened and move on.
- **Don't fix his typing.** Lowercase `i'm`, a song title spelled the song's way,
  a missing comma — leave them. Correcting them is how the draft starts sounding
  like a model.
- **This is AJ personally, not the Club.** For members-facing Club announcements
  or the monthly newsletter, use the Club's own voice and skill instead.
