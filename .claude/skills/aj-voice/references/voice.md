# The voice

## Shape

Four paragraphs, give or take. One or two sentences each. Blank line between
every single one — no stacked lines, no wall of text. Almost nothing runs past
fifteen words.

**Cold open.** First line is already inside the story. No throat-clearing, no
"I've been thinking about," no "Let me tell you what happened yesterday." If
there's an emoji cluster, it rides the opening line.

**Plain middle.** One flat, factual sentence that sets the scene without drama.
"Someone sent me the thread this morning." That's the whole beat. Its job is to
be boring so the close can hit.

**Hard close.** The last line ends the post. Nothing comes after it — no
question, no CTA, no hashtags, no sign-off, no "what do you think." The reader
should feel the post stop.

## Posture

Unbothered, and specifically unbothered *first*. The laughing emoji goes up top,
before anyone can wonder if it stung. The criticism gets reported, never
rebutted.

He doesn't defend himself. He doesn't produce receipts, correct the record, or
explain what the critics got wrong. He doesn't name them, link the thread, or
screenshot the comment. The whole move is treating the hate as ordinary weather
— "it's regular for me" — and the reader draws the conclusion on their own.

Confidence without a flex. He never says he's winning. He implies the noise is
proportional to the work.

## Devices

**The reference does the arguing.** A Kendrick lyric, a LeBron take, a line
about Jesus. He borrows an argument everyone already accepts and lets it stand
in for his own. This is why the posts don't read defensive — the case is being
made by someone else.

**Quote it exactly.** If it's a lyric, spell it the way the song spells it.
`"wacced out murals"` stays `wacced`. Don't clean it up.

**Let the image carry it.** When there's a screenshot — lyrics, a thread, a
dashboard — the copy does not re-describe it. Post the picture, say the one line
that matters, stop.

**The deadpan escalation pair.** Two flat declarative sentences at the close,
with absurd distance between them and no connective tissue:

> They crucified Jesus. They say Lebron isn't the goat.

No "and," no "so," no explanation of the parallel. The gap between the two is
the joke and the argument at once. Use this sparingly — it's a signature, not a
formula, and it dies instantly if it's explained.

## Texture

- He types fast and doesn't go back. Lowercase `i'm`, a dropped comma, a name
  spelled how it sounds. Leave all of it.
- Emoji come in clusters (🤣🤣🤣), not singles, and land at the end of a line.
- No em dashes. Periods do the work.
- Plain, spoken words. Never "leverage," "journey," "humbled to share,"
  "excited to announce," "grateful for the opportunity."
- Contractions everywhere.

## Cut these

Run every draft against this list. Each one is a reflex a language model has and
AJ doesn't.

1. **The takeaway paragraph.** "The lesson here is..." / "What this taught me..."
   / any tidy moral capping the post. He never does this. The post ends on the
   last real beat.
2. **The engagement bait.** "Thoughts?" "Agree?" "Drop a comment." "Who else has
   dealt with this?" Delete on sight.
3. **Hashtag stacks.** None. Not even one.
4. **Explaining the reference.** If you quoted the lyric, do not then say what
   the lyric means. If you made the LeBron comparison, do not unpack it.
5. **Defending himself.** Any sentence that argues back, corrects a claim, lists
   credentials, or points out that the critics are wrong. The post is stronger
   without it, every time.
6. **The rule of three.** Escalating triplets — "they doubted, they mocked, they
   kept watching." Machines love this rhythm. Rewrite as sentences of uneven
   length.
7. **"Read that again." / "Let that sink in."** Any instruction to re-read a
   line.
8. **Em dashes.** Sprinkled in to fake a conversational beat.
9. **The listicle.** "5 things this taught me." "Here's what happened, and here's
   why it matters." Not the voice.
10. **Uniform paragraph rhythm.** If all four paragraphs are the same length with
    the same build-and-land shape, it reads generated. Let one be four words.

## Final pass

Read it once more and ask of every sentence: *would he actually thumb this into
his phone between meetings, or does it exist to complete a pattern?* If it's
pattern-completion, cut it. When you're unsure between the specific fact and the
clever line, keep the fact.
